# Write learning notes

The reader should be able to explain the video's problem, required concepts, their relationships, the creator's example, and where the explanation applies. Follow the evidence/coverage rules in [Distillation](distillation.md).

Start with a concrete task from the video: its inputs, important action, observable result, and why it matters. Organize around complete questions, such as why a step is needed or how one component affects another. Use the preceding result to explain why the next part follows.

Explain unfamiliar terms near their first use and point to what they mean in the running example. Preserve the creator's original example and track intermediate states of the same data, code, diagram, or project. Label invented analogies/examples as illustrative additions, not video content.

Build causal understanding rather than a glossary or checklist: example/observation, mechanism, then relevant terms, formulas and conditions. Put essential prerequisites and reasoning in the main text; reserve optional depth for later. Do not impose a fixed number of sections, exercises, or decorative elements.

Do not invent missing settings, commands, versions, or actions. Distinguish source content, explanation, inference and verification. Make limitations specific: stated applicability, source date, and claims narrated without demonstration. End by returning to the original task: what can now be understood or attempted, what remains unresolved, and what evidence is missing.

A note commonly connects the core question, the running example, necessary concepts, intermediate changes, key visuals actually inspected, conditions, and remaining gaps. It need not give each a separate heading. Reply in the requested language, otherwise the conversation language; preserve exact source commands/identifiers.

Choose prose or a simple diagram to explain the actual difficulty. Create a webpage/interactive demonstration only if requested and verify it if created. Reading notes is not proof of mastery. Deep tutoring, grading, revision schedules and learning-state tracking are separate tasks. A handoff can include source identity, paths, timestamps, coverage, original example and gaps. A few requested self-check questions may adapt the video's example with provenance; do not automatically research a separate question bank.
